# Mathematics — Primary 1 to 6

Source: MOE *Mathematics Syllabus Primary One to Six*, 2021 Primary 1 cohort, updated Oct 2025. **That PDF is not shipped with the skill.** The `(pNN)` markers in the level files record which of its pages a claim came from — provenance for anyone auditing against the source, not a pointer to follow. Everything needed to fix a pitch, teach, set, mark and answer "what does this level cover" is in this file and the level files it routes to; where they are silent, tell the user it is not in the source rather than filling the gap.

The syllabus carries its own phase-in note: in 2025 it "applies to Primary 1 to Primary 5 only. Students in Primary 6 will continue to use the 2013 syllabus. The 2021 mathematics syllabus will be applicable to Primary 6 from 2026 onwards" (p30). From 2026 the P6 content in the level files is the live one for every cohort.

P1-P4 is common to all students. At P5-P6 the syllabus splits: **Standard** continues P1-4, while **Foundation** revisits key P1-4 content and takes a subset of the new Standard material.

Content sits in three strands, each divided into sub-strands:

| Strand | Sub-strands |
|---|---|
| Number and Algebra | Whole Numbers, Fractions, Decimals, Money, Percentage, Rate, Ratio, Algebra |
| Measurement and Geometry | Measurement, Area and Volume, Geometry |
| Statistics | Data Representation and Interpretation, Data Analysis |

Under each sub-strand sit numbered topics ("1. Numbers up to 100"), and under each topic numbered learning outcomes (1.1, 1.2 …). Not every sub-strand appears at every level — Percentage starts at P5, Ratio and Algebra only at Standard P6, and Standard P5 has no Statistics sub-strand at all. The level files name the sub-strand and topic for each item, in the syllabus's own wording, so quote those when answering a look-up question.

## How to teach it

The curriculum's centre is **problem solving**, supported by concepts, skills, processes, metacognition and attitudes (p13). Aim at *relational* understanding — "knowing the why, not just the what and how" (Skemp, quoted p20) — over *instrumental* understanding, the what and how alone. The 2021 revision's three stated focus areas are the critical mathematical processes, awareness of the **big ideas**, and greater emphasis on metacognition (p5); the big ideas named are Diagrams, Equivalence, Invariance, Measures, Notations and Proportionality (p11-12), which is the vocabulary to reach for when a child asks why two topics behave alike.

**Scaffold concrete → pictorial → abstract.** The pictorial stage is where Singapore maths does its work, and the **bar model** (model method) is the main representation: part-whole bars for addition, subtraction and fractions-of-a-whole; comparison bars for difference, ratio and percentage. Draw the bars before writing the equation, at every level where the problem is a word problem.

Both of those are standard Singapore classroom practice rather than quoted syllabus — this edition never uses the phrase *concrete-pictorial-abstract* and never names the bar model or the model method. What it does say is that "diagrams are succinct, visual representations of real-world or mathematical objects that serve to communicate properties of the objects and facilitate problem solving" (p11), and it names three lesson phases — Readiness, Engagement, Mastery — with activity-based learning, inquiry-based learning and direct instruction as the pedagogies (p19). So teach with bars; just don't attribute them to MOE.

Reach for the bar model and not algebra for every word problem up to P5. Letters for unknowns are a **P6** topic; using `x` at P4 is above-level even when it is faster.

Commonly taught heuristics — draw a diagram, make a list, guess and check, work backwards, look for a pattern, simplify the problem, act it out, before-after comparison. These are standard Singapore classroom practice rather than a list the 2021 syllabus itself enumerates; the syllabus names Pólya's four steps (understand, plan, carry out, look back) and "the use of heuristics" (p13) and stops there.

**Word problems — "problem sums" — have their own named concepts**, and a child is taught to recognise which one a question is asking for: remainder/branching, equal fractions, model drawing, constant part, constant total, constant difference, everything changed, excess and shortage, difference, grouping, number × value, assumption, units and parts, working backwards, double if. Read [`math-problem-sums.md`](math-problem-sums.md), when it ships with this skill, for tutoring, setting or marking a word problem, diagnosing a child who scores Paper 1 and crashes Paper 2, or being asked how to help a child practise: it carries the keyword-to-concept mapping, the method and a worked example for each concept, and the guiding questions that hand a step back to the child. Like the heuristics above it is classroom practice, not quoted syllabus; this file stays the authority on what each level covers. Where it is absent, name the concept from the list above and work it with the heuristics and the bar model.

## Marking conventions

Two of these come from the syllabus itself: money "in decimal notation" from P2, and `≈` with "rounding answers to a specified degree of accuracy" from P4. The rest is standard Singapore exam convention — apply it, but do not quote it as MOE.

- Method marks are real. Correct working in the taught method earns credit with a wrong final answer.
- Units are part of the answer — `$4.50`, `12 cm`, `350 mℓ`. A bare number where a unit is required loses the mark.
- Money is written in decimal notation from P2 (`$4.05`, not `$4.5`).
- Answers are rounded only to the accuracy asked for; `≈` is introduced at P4.
- Statement answers in word problems are expected to name what was found.

## Calculator use

The syllabus says nothing general about calculators. What it does is tag individual learning outcomes "without calculator", and those tags are the only calculator information in the document — so never tell a user that a calculator is allowed at some level, and treat a tagged skill as one the child must be able to do by hand.

Where the tag falls:

- **P1-P4**: no outcome carries it.
- **P5 Standard**: ×/÷ by 10, 100, 1000 and their multiples; order of operations; use of brackets; proper/improper fraction × whole number; proper fraction × proper/improper fraction; ×/÷ of decimals (up to 3 dp) by 10, 100, 1000 and their multiples. Not tagged: improper × improper, mixed number × whole number.
- **P6 Standard**: dividing a proper fraction by a whole number; dividing a whole number or proper fraction by a proper fraction.
- **P5 Foundation**: + and − algorithms (up to 3 digits); × and ÷ algorithms (up to 2 digits by 1 digit); ×/÷ by 10, 100, 1000 and their multiples; adding and subtracting fractions; proper/improper fraction × whole number; proper fraction × proper/improper fraction; adding and subtracting decimals (up to 2 dp); ×/÷ of decimals (up to 3 dp) by 10, 100, 1000.
- **P6 Foundation**: dividing a whole number by a whole number with quotient as a decimal.

## Setting a paper

The syllabus prescribes **no paper format** — no booklets, no question counts, no mark totals, no duration. When a user wants a paper shaped like their school's, ask for or follow their own paper, and say the format is not something the syllabus sets.

What the syllabus does say about building a test (p27-28):

- Every summative assessment "should have a Table of Specification (TOS)" giving the relative distribution of topics and the cognitive levels of the items, so that "no topics is overly assessed in the same test paper". Spread the questions across the level's sub-strands rather than loading one.
- "The overall difficulty level of the paper must be carefully planned, with an appropriate distribution of easy, moderate and difficult items. An appropriately pitched paper allows students to experience success and gives a fair reflection of the effectiveness of teaching and learning."
- "All summative assessment must be appropriately pitched according to the syllabus." Above-level testing "may result in loss of motivation for learning, anxiety and work avoidance amongst students" — which is the syllabus's own argument for the range limits the level files carry.

Classroom practice, not syllabus: most Singapore primary papers run as Paper 1 (multiple-choice and short answer, worked without a calculator) and Paper 2 (short answer plus structured word problems worth 2-5 marks each). Offer that shape as a default and confirm it against whatever the child actually sits.

## Standard vs Foundation, for "is X in or out"

Standard-only across P5-P6, and therefore out of any Foundation paper: ratio; algebra; the circle, semicircle and quarter circle; percentage increase/decrease and finding the whole from a part; properties and angles of parallelogram, rhombus and trapezium; reverse volume problems with √ and ∛; multiplying two improper fractions; multiplying a mixed number and a whole number; decimal ×/÷ "and their multiples" of 10, 100, 1000.

Foundation-only in effect: nothing new in content, but the Foundation P5 revisit of P4 number work is explicit syllabus content at P5, so a Foundation P5 paper may legitimately be built on it; and "multiplying and dividing decimals" without a digit restriction is listed at Foundation P6 and nowhere in Standard.

Same in both streams: fraction division at P6; area of triangle; volume of a cube/cuboid and of liquid in a tank; triangle properties and angle sum; average; rate; discount, GST and annual interest.

## Progression chains worth knowing

Useful when diagnosing a gap, because the break is usually one level below the topic on the page.

- **Fractions**: P2 part of a whole, like/unit fractions → P3 equivalent fractions, simplest form, unlike comparison, related addition → P4 mixed/improper, part of a set, two different denominators → P5 mixed-number addition, multiplication, fraction quotients → P6 division by a fraction.
- **Decimals**: P2 money in decimal notation → P4 up to 3 dp, four operations with 1-digit multipliers → P5 ×/÷ by powers of 10, unit conversion in decimal form → P6 (Foundation) general multiplication and division.
- **Proportionality**: P5 percentage and rate → P6 percentage increase/decrease, finding the whole, ratio → secondary algebra. Bar models carry all of it.
- **Area and volume**: P3 area/perimeter of rectangle and square → P4 one dimension from the other, composite figures → P5 triangle, volume of cube/cuboid → P6 circle, reverse volume problems.
- **Angles**: P3 compared to a right angle → P4 measured in degrees, drawn → P5 straight line, point, vertically opposite, triangle, quadrilaterals → P6 composite figures.
- **Whole numbers**: P1 to 100 → P2 to 1000 → P3 to 10 000 → P4 to 100 000 with rounding and factors/multiples → P5 to 10 million, powers of 10, order of operations and brackets.
- **Multiplication and division**: P1 within 40 / within 20 → P2 tables of 2-5 and 10 → P3 tables of 6-9, remainder, 3 digits by 1 digit → P4 4 digits by 1 digit and 3 digits by 2 digits → P5 by 10, 100, 1000 and their multiples.
