# Mathematics — Primary 5 and 6 (Foundation)

Quoted from MOE *Mathematics Syllabus Primary One to Six*, 2021 Primary 1 cohort, updated Oct 2025, which is not shipped with the skill; the `(pNN)` markers record which of its pages a claim came from rather than pointing anywhere you need to go. How to teach it, the marking conventions, the calculator tags, paper-setting, the Standard-vs-Foundation summary and the progression chains are in [`../math.md`](../math.md).

Foundation revisits key P1-P4 content and takes a subset of the new Standard material. The Standard levels are in [`p5-p6-standard.md`](p5-p6-standard.md).

## Primary 5 (Foundation)

**Number and Algebra** — *Whole Numbers, 1. Numbers up to 10 million:* reading and writing numbers in numerals and in words; comparing and ordering numbers up to 100 000; rounding numbers to the nearest 10, 100 or 1000; patterns in number sequences; use of ≈. *2. Four Operations:* addition and subtraction algorithms (up to 3 digits) without calculator; multiplication and division algorithms (up to 2 digits by 1 digit) without calculator; multiplying and dividing by 10, 100, 1000 and their multiples without calculator; order of operations; use of brackets; mental calculation involving addition and subtraction of a 3-digit number and ones/tens/hundreds, and multiplication and division within the multiplication tables. *3. Factors and Multiples:* factors, multiples and their relationship; determining if a 1-digit number is a factor of a given number within 100; finding the common factors of two given numbers; determining if a number is a multiple of a given 1-digit number; finding the common multiples of two given 1-digit numbers. *Fractions, 1. Concepts of Fractions:* fraction as part of a whole; fraction as part of a set. *2. Equivalent Fractions:* equivalent fractions; expressing a fraction in its simplest form; comparing and ordering unlike fractions, with denominators of given fractions not exceeding 12; writing the equivalent fraction of a fraction given the denominator or the numerator. *3. Mixed Numbers and Improper Fractions:* mixed numbers and improper fractions and their relationships. *4. Four Operations:* adding and subtracting fractions, denominators not exceeding 12 and not more than two different denominators, without calculator; adding and subtracting mixed numbers; multiplying a proper/improper fraction and a whole number without calculator; multiplying a proper fraction and a proper/improper fraction without calculator. (p45)

*Decimals, 1. Decimals up to 3 decimal places:* notation, representations and place values (tenths, hundredths, thousandths); comparing and ordering decimals; expressing decimals as fractions; expressing fractions as decimals when the denominator is a factor of 10 or 100; rounding decimals to the nearest whole number, 1 decimal place, 2 decimal places. *2. Four operations:* adding and subtracting decimals (up to 2 decimal places) without calculator; multiplying and dividing decimals (up to 3 decimal places) by 10, 100, 1000 without calculator; converting a measurement from a smaller unit to a larger unit in decimal form and vice versa, for km and m, m and cm, kg and g, ℓ and mℓ. *Rate, 1. Rate:* rate as the amount of a quantity per unit of another quantity; finding rate, total amount or number of units given the other two quantities. (p46)

**Measurement and Geometry** — *Measurement, 1. Time:* measuring time in hours and minutes; converting time in hours and minutes to minutes only and vice versa; finding the starting time, finishing time or duration given the other two quantities; 24-hour clock. *Area and Volume, 1. Area and Perimeter:* concept of area and perimeter of a plane figure; measuring area in square units, cm² and m², "excluding conversion between cm² and m²"; area and perimeter of rectangle/square; finding one dimension of a rectangle given the other dimension and its area/perimeter; finding the length of one side of a square given its area/perimeter; finding the area and perimeter of composite figures made up of rectangles and squares. *2. Volume of Cube and Cuboid:* building solids with unit cubes; measuring volume in cubic units, cm³ and m³, "excluding conversion between cm³ and m³"; drawing cubes and cuboids on isometric grid. *Geometry, 1. Perpendicular and Parallel Lines:* perpendicular and parallel lines; drawing perpendicular and parallel lines. *2. Angles:* using notation such as ∠ABC and ∠a to name angles; measuring angles in degrees; drawing an angle of a given size; angles on a straight line; angles at a point; vertically opposite angles; finding unknown angles. *3. Rectangle and Square:* properties of rectangle and square, "excluding diagonal properties"; drawing rectangle and square. (p47)

**Statistics** — *Data Representation and Interpretation, 1. Tables, Bar Graphs and Line Graphs:* reading and interpreting data from tables/graphs; completing a table from given data. (p48)

Boundaries at P5 Foundation:

- "Measuring area in square units, cm² and m², **excluding conversion between cm² and m²**"; "measuring volume in cubic units, cm³ and m³ **excluding conversion between cm³ and m³**"; "properties of rectangle and square, **excluding diagonal properties**".
- No percentage at Foundation P5 — it arrives at Foundation P6.
- Volume stops at building solids, measuring in cubic units and drawing on isometric grid. The volume formula for a cube or cuboid, volume of liquid in a tank, and the ℓ-to-cm³ relationship are Foundation P6.
- No area of triangle at Foundation P5 (Foundation P6).
- Unknown angles are limited to angles on a straight line, angles at a point and vertically opposite angles. Triangle properties and angle sum of a triangle are Foundation P6; parallelogram, rhombus and trapezium never appear at Foundation.
- Fraction multiplication stops at proper/improper × whole number and proper × proper/improper. Improper × improper, mixed number × whole number, and any division of fractions are not here — the first two are Standard P5, fraction division is P6.
- Decimal ×/÷ is "by 10, 100, 1000" — note the Standard P5 wording adds "and their multiples" and the Foundation wording does not.
- Whole numbers: reading and writing goes to 10 million, but "comparing and ordering numbers **up to 100 000**".
- The four-operation algorithms are smaller than Standard: + and − "up to 3 digits", × and ÷ "up to 2 digits by 1 digit".
- Foundation P5 revisits P4's whole numbers, factors, fractions, decimals, time, area and perimeter and geometry. What it does not revisit — money, nets, 2D representations of solids, line symmetry, pie charts — was covered at P4 and can be assumed as prior learning, but is not re-taught at this level and should not be the focus of a Foundation P5 paper.

## Primary 6 (Foundation)

**Number and Algebra** — *Fractions, 1. Fraction and Division:* dividing a whole number by a whole number with quotient as a fraction; expressing fractions as decimals. *2. Four Operations:* dividing a proper fraction by a whole number; dividing a whole number / proper fraction by a proper fraction. *Decimals, 1. Multiplication and Division:* multiplying and dividing decimals; dividing a whole number by a whole number with quotient as a decimal without calculator; rounding answers to a specified degree of accuracy. *Percentage, 1. Percentage:* expressing a part of a whole as a percentage; use of %; finding a percentage part of a whole; finding discount, GST and annual interest. (p49)

**Measurement and Geometry** — *Area and Volume, 1. Area of Triangle:* concepts of base and height of a triangle; area of triangle; finding the area and perimeter of composite figures made up of squares, rectangles and triangles. *2. Volume of cube and cuboid:* volume of a cube/cuboid; finding the volume of liquid in a rectangular tank, "excluding conversion between cm³ and m³"; relationship between ℓ (or mℓ) with cm³. *Geometry, 1. Rectangle, square and triangle:* properties of isosceles triangle, equilateral triangle and right-angled triangle; angle sum of a triangle; finding unknown angles, "without additional construction of lines", in composite geometric figures involving square, rectangle and triangle. (p49)

**Statistics** — *Data Representation and Interpretation, 1. Pie Charts:* reading and interpreting data from pie charts. *Data Analysis, 1. Average of a set of data:* average as 'total value ÷ number of data'; relationship between average, total value and number of data. (p50)

Boundaries at P6 Foundation:

- "Finding the volume of liquid in a rectangular tank **excluding conversion between cm³ and m³**"; "finding unknown angles, **without additional construction of lines**".
- No ratio and no algebra anywhere at Foundation. Every ratio- or algebra-based word-problem method is out of reach here — recast it as a model.
- No circle work at all: area and circumference of circle, semicircle and quarter circle are Standard P6 only.
- Percentage at Foundation P6 is exactly the Standard P5 set — part as a percentage, a percentage part of a whole, discount, GST and annual interest. **Finding the whole given a part and the percentage, and percentage increase/decrease, are not at Foundation.**
- Composite figures involve squares, rectangles and triangles only. No parallelogram, rhombus or trapezium at Foundation, for area or for angles.
- Reverse volume work — one dimension from the volume, the edge of a cube from its volume, use of √ and ∛ — is Standard P6 only.
- "Multiplying and dividing decimals" is listed with no digit limit at Foundation P6, which is the one place decimal × decimal appears in the primary syllabus; the "without calculator" tag sits on the whole-number division with a decimal quotient, not on it.
- Pie charts are "reading and interpreting" only, even though percentage is available at this level.
- Foundation P6 adds no new Whole Numbers or Measurement content.
