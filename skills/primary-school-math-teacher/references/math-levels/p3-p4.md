# Mathematics — Primary 3 and 4

Quoted from MOE *Mathematics Syllabus Primary One to Six*, 2021 Primary 1 cohort, updated Oct 2025, which is not shipped with the skill; the `(pNN)` markers record which of its pages a claim came from rather than pointing anywhere you need to go. How to teach it, the marking conventions, the calculator tags, paper-setting, the Standard-vs-Foundation summary and the progression chains are in [`../math.md`](../math.md).

Both levels are common to all students; the Standard / Foundation split starts at P5.

## Primary 3

**Number and Algebra** — *Whole Numbers, 1. Numbers up to 10 000:* counting in hundreds/thousands; number notation, representations and place values (thousands, hundreds, tens, ones); numerals and words; comparing and ordering numbers; patterns in number sequences. *2. Addition and Subtraction:* addition and subtraction algorithms (up to 4 digits); mental calculation of two 2-digit numbers. *3. Multiplication and Division:* multiplication tables of 6, 7, 8 and 9; multiplying and dividing within the multiplication tables; division with remainder; multiplication and division algorithms (up to 3 digits by 1 digit); mental calculation within the multiplication tables. *Fractions, 1. Equivalent fractions:* equivalent fractions; expressing a fraction in its simplest form; comparing and ordering unlike fractions, denominators not exceeding 12; writing the equivalent fraction of a fraction given the denominator or the numerator. *2. Addition and Subtraction:* adding and subtracting two related fractions within one whole, denominators not exceeding 12. *Money, 1. Money:* adding and subtracting money in decimal notation. (p35)

**Measurement and Geometry** — *Measurement, 1. Length, Mass and Volume:* measuring length in kilometres (km) and volume of liquid in millilitres (mℓ); measuring length / mass / volume of liquid in compound units; converting a measurement in compound units to the smaller unit and vice versa, for km and m, m and cm, kg and g, ℓ and mℓ — "numbers involved should be within easy manipulation". *2. Time:* measuring time in seconds; finding the starting time, finishing time or duration given the other two quantities; 24-hour clock. *Area and Volume, 1. Area and Perimeter:* concepts of area and perimeter of a plane figure; measuring area in square units, cm² and m², "excluding conversion between cm² and m²"; perimeter of rectilinear figure, rectangle and square; area of rectangle/square. *Geometry, 1. Angles:* concepts of angle; right angles, angles greater than / smaller than a right angle. *2. Perpendicular and Parallel Lines:* perpendicular and parallel lines; drawing perpendicular and parallel lines. (p36)

**Statistics** — *Data Representation and Interpretation, 1. Bar Graphs:* reading and interpreting data from bar graphs; using different scales on axis. (p36)

Boundaries at P3:

- Area: "measuring area in square units, cm² and m², **excluding conversion between cm² and m²**".
- Compound-unit conversion: "numbers involved should be within easy manipulation" — keep the conversions clean rather than testing arithmetic under cover of a unit change.
- Fraction addition and subtraction is "two related fractions within one whole" — one denominator a multiple of the other, exactly two fractions, and no answer above 1. Unrelated denominators are P4.
- Comparing and ordering reaches unlike fractions; the four operations do not follow it there.
- Angles are compared to a right angle. The notation ∠ABC, measuring in degrees and drawing a given angle are P4.
- Area formulas stop at rectangle and square. Triangle area is P5; composite figures are P4.
- Multiplication and division algorithms are up to 3 digits by 1 digit; 4-digit division and 2-digit multipliers are P4.
- Decimals are still money-only — the decimals sub-strand opens at P4.

## Primary 4

**Number and Algebra** — *Whole Numbers, 1. Numbers up to 100 000:* number notation, representations and place values (ten thousands, thousands, hundreds, tens, ones); numerals and words; comparing and ordering numbers; patterns in number sequences; rounding numbers to the nearest 10, 100 or 1000; use of ≈. *2. Factors and Multiples:* factors, multiples and their relationship; determining if a 1-digit number is a factor of a given number within 100; finding the common factors of two given numbers; determining if a number is a multiple of a given 1-digit number; finding the common multiples of two given 1-digit numbers. *3. Four Operations:* multiplication algorithm — up to 4 digits by 1 digit, and up to 3 digits by 2 digits; division algorithm (up to 4 digits by 1 digit). *Fractions, 1. Mixed Numbers and Improper Fractions:* mixed numbers, improper fractions and their relationship. *2. Fraction of a Set:* fraction as part of a set. *3. Addition and Subtraction:* adding and subtracting fractions "with denominators of given fractions not exceeding 12 and not more than two different denominators". (p37)

*Decimals, 1. Decimals up to 3 decimal places:* notation, representations and place values (tenths, hundredths, thousandths); comparing and ordering decimals; expressing decimals as fractions; expressing fractions as decimals when the denominator is a factor of 10 or 100; rounding decimals to the nearest whole number, 1 decimal place, 2 decimal places. *2. Addition and Subtraction:* adding and subtracting decimals (up to 2 decimal places). *3. Multiplication and Division:* multiplying and dividing decimals (up to 2 decimal places) by a 1-digit whole number; dividing a whole number by a whole number with quotient as a decimal; rounding answers to a specified degree of accuracy. (p38)

**Measurement and Geometry** — *Area and Volume, 1. Area and Perimeter:* finding one dimension of a rectangle given the other dimension and its area/perimeter; finding the length of one side of a square given its area/perimeter; finding the area and perimeter of composite figures made up of rectangles and squares. *Geometry, 1. Angles:* using notation such as ∠ABC and ∠a to name angles; measuring angles in degrees; drawing an angle of given size. *2. Rectangle and Square:* properties of rectangle and square, "excluding diagonal properties"; drawing rectangles and squares. *3. Line Symmetry:* identifying symmetric figures; determining whether a straight line is a line of symmetry of a symmetric figure; completing a symmetric figure with respect to a given line of symmetry on square grid. *4. Nets:* identifying 2D representations of cube, cuboid, cone, cylinder, prism, pyramid; drawing 2D representations of cube, cuboid, prism, pyramid; identifying the nets of cube, cuboid, prism, pyramid; identifying the solid which can be formed by a given net. (p39)

**Statistics** — *Data Representation and Interpretation, 1. Tables, Line Graphs and Pie Charts:* completing a table from given data; reading and interpreting data from tables / line graphs / pie charts. (p40)

Boundaries at P4:

- Rectangle and square: "properties of rectangle and square, **excluding diagonal properties**".
- Fractions: "with denominators of given fractions not exceeding 12 **and not more than two different denominators**". Multiplying and dividing fractions are P5 and P6.
- Factors and multiples: "determining if a **1-digit** number is a factor of a given number **within 100**", and "common multiples of two given **1-digit** numbers". Prime numbers, prime factorisation, and the terms HCF and LCM are not in the primary syllabus at all.
- Decimals: multiplying and dividing is "(up to 2 decimal places) **by a 1-digit whole number**" only. Decimal by decimal, and ×/÷ by 10, 100, 1000, are later.
- Line symmetry is completed "on square grid"; rotational symmetry is not in the syllabus.
- 2D representations are drawn for cube, cuboid, prism and pyramid — the cone and cylinder are identified only, not drawn. Nets are identified, not drawn.
- Pie charts are "reading and interpreting" only — drawing one, or computing a share from an angle or a percentage, is not listed, and percentage itself is P5.
- No area of triangle and no volume of cube/cuboid at P4; both are P5.
