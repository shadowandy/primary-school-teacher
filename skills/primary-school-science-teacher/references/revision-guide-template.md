# Revision guide template — topical notes

The house format for a revision guide or topical notes on one topic: what a child revises from and a parent can teach off.

It is a **formatting convention, not MOE's** — the syllabus prescribes no notes format. `science.md` stays the authority on content, on every boundary the guide is written inside, and on the command words the answering frame is marked to; `science-answering-techniques.md`, when it ships with this skill, routes to the frames for the topic, and where it is absent the causal chain in `science.md` does.

## How to fill it

- **One topic, one level, one stream.** Pair the theme with the level — a theme reappears at several levels with a different topic each time — and write a second level as its own guide.
- **The boundary warning is the first thing written and the hardest line to get right.** Quote the "not required" statement close to verbatim and name the term an adult reaches past it for, with the in-boundary phrasing beside it: *lets most light through* for *translucent*, *food-carrying tubes* for *phloem*, *potential energy* for *elastic potential energy*.
- **Vocabulary table**: the term as the syllabus words it, a plain-language meaning inside the boundary, and the specific thing children confuse it with — melting against dissolving, a food web's arrow direction, cold as a substance that flows in.
- **Concrete before abstract** in the breakdown, and prefer the representation the child is taught in: a labelled diagram, the two set-ups being compared, a classification rule.
- **One worked example** with real data, answered in the full chain rather than the final claim, honouring the command word the question used.
- **Teacher diagnosis last**: name the misconception from the misconception file rather than calling the answer careless, and say which link was missing.

## The template

```markdown
# [Level] Science: [Topic Name] – Revision Guide

**Level**: Primary [3–6] | **Stream**: [Standard / Foundation] | **Syllabus**: MOE [Year] Cohort  
**Key Focus**: [Theme / Topic / Core idea]  
**Boundary Warning**: [What is OUT at this level, close to the syllabus's own wording — e.g. "The terms transparent / translucent / opaque are not required. Say 'lets most light through'."]

---

## 1. Key Concept Summary & Vocabulary

*Define core terms using exact MOE vocabulary for the level.*

| Term / Concept | Meaning & Classroom Definition | Common Trap / Misconception |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **[Term 1]** | [Plain-language definition inside the boundary] | [What children commonly confuse it with] |
| **[Term 2]** | [Plain-language definition inside the boundary] | [What children commonly confuse it with] |

### Core Concept Breakdown
- **[Sub-topic A]**: [Scaffolded bullet points; concrete before abstract]
- **[Sub-topic B]**: [Classification rules, properties, or the labelled parts]

---

## 2. Answering Frame (Claim–Evidence–Reasoning)

1. **Claim (C)**: State the direct observation or choice clearly, answering the command word the question used.
2. **Evidence (E)**: Cite the exact comparative data from the table or diagram — *"bent 8.5 cm, which is further than the 4.0 cm of Strip B"*.
3. **Reasoning (R)**: Link to the scientific principle — *"Materials that bend more under the same load are more flexible"*.

Keep the comparative word in the answer; a chain that drops it loses the mark even when the science is right.

---

## 3. Quick Check & Worked Example

**Example Problem**:  
[Question with a set-up, a table of readings or a diagram, inside the boundary]

**Step-by-Step Solution & Teacher Notes**:
- **Model Answer**: [The full chain, C then E then R, in the frames the child is taught]
- **Teacher Diagnosis**: [The pitfall this topic produces — e.g. "States the result without citing the comparison", or a named misconception such as "Treats cold as a substance that flows in"]
```

## Check the guide against itself

Read the model answer back against the boundary warning you wrote at the top. A guide whose own answer needs a term the warning rules out is the common failure here, and it is also the most convincing version of the error — the child learns the out-of-syllabus word from the page meant to keep them off it.
