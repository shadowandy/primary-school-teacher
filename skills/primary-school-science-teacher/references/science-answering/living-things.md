# Science frames — plants, digestion, circulation and respiration

Themes: Cycles (reproduction), Energy (photosynthesis), Systems (plant and human systems).

The five moves every answer makes, the boundary notes and the pitch are in [`../science-answering-techniques.md`](../science-answering-techniques.md); the misconceptions to name when marking are in [`misconceptions.md`](misconceptions.md).

## Reproduction in plants

- **Animal pollination** — *When the animal collects nectar, pollen grains from the anthers are transferred to its body. When it visits another flower, the pollen grains on its body are transferred to the stigma of that flower, resulting in pollination*.
- **Flower to fruit** — *The stigma receives pollen grains, resulting in pollination. The male reproductive cells from the pollen grains then fuse with the female reproductive cells in the ovules, resulting in fertilisation. The ovary then develops into a fruit*. It is the **ovary** that develops into the fruit; "the flower develops into a fruit" is marked down where the question says *explain clearly*.
- **Can this flower bear fruit** — it needs a stigma to receive pollen grains and an ovary for fertilisation.
- **Animal- or wind-pollinated** — animal: large brightly coloured petals, nectar or scent, anthers and stigma inside the flower so visitors brush against them, sticky stigma. Wind: small dull petals, little nectar or scent, anthers hanging out so wind carries pollen away, large feathery stigma with more surface area to catch pollen from the air.
- **Why dispersal matters** — to prevent overcrowding and competition for resources such as sunlight, nutrients and water.

## Photosynthesis and energy in food

- **Photosynthesis** — *The plant absorbs light energy using its chlorophyll, and uses carbon dioxide and water to photosynthesise. Food and oxygen are produced*. More light → faster photosynthesis → more oxygen produced: *the plant absorbs more light, so its rate of photosynthesis increases and more oxygen is produced*. Write *sunlight* only where the stem's light source is the Sun; a lamp gives *light*.
- **Respiration** — *Living things use digested food and oxygen to carry out respiration. Energy is released and carbon dioxide is released as a waste product*.
- **Plant and animal in a sealed container, in the day** — the plant photosynthesises and releases oxygen; the animal takes in that oxygen for respiration and releases carbon dioxide; the plant takes in that carbon dioxide to photosynthesise again. Plants respire as well as photosynthesise; animals only respire.

## Digestion

- *Digestive juice breaks food **down** into simpler substances*; *chewing breaks food **up** into smaller pieces*. Keep the two verbs and the two nouns distinct — "break down into smaller pieces" blurs them. Glucose and digested food are the **simpler substances**; starch and undigested food are what is broken down.
- **Why chewing or churning speeds digestion** — breaking food up into smaller pieces and mixing it with digestive juices gives the food a greater surface area for the digestive juices to act on.

## Circulatory and respiratory systems

- **Transport** — *Blood transports oxygen, digested food and water to the body cells*, and *transports waste substances and carbon dioxide to the organs for removal from the body*. Blood transports; it does not remove. Blood travels inside blood vessels.
- **At the cells** — *Body cells use digested food and oxygen to carry out respiration to release energy for life processes. Carbon dioxide is released as a waste product*.
- **Heart rate rising with activity** — *A strenuous activity requires more energy. The heart pumps faster to circulate blood so more oxygen and digested food reach the muscle cells for faster respiration, releasing more energy. Faster respiration also produces more waste carbon dioxide, and the increased circulation carries it to the lungs more quickly for removal*.

Any organ is made of living cells respiring all the time, so blood leaving an organ carries more carbon dioxide than blood entering it. Gaseous exchange happens in the lungs — not in the organ the question is about.
