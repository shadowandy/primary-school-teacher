# Science — the recurring misconceptions

Open when marking or diagnosing an open-ended Science script. The rows run in the same topic order as the frame files: heat and matter, then plants and body systems, then adaptation, electricity, energy, photosynthesis and the environment.

The five moves every answer makes, the boundary notes and the pitch are in [`../science-answering-techniques.md`](../science-answering-techniques.md), which also routes to the frames topic by topic.

## Marking: the recurring misconceptions

Name the pattern, not the slip. These are the ones that recur in real scripts, with what to write back.

| What the child wrote | The misconception |
|---|---|
| "The steel plate is colder than the glass plate" | Confuses temperature with rate of heat transfer — both plates left the same refrigerator |
| "Steel is a better conductor, so her hand feels colder on the glass plate" | Right concept, conclusion reversed, and *conducts heat from* never written |
| "Steel is a better conductor, so more water evaporates" | Chain broken: no link from the material to the water gaining heat |
| "The exposed surface area of the cup is larger" | Attributes evaporation to the container, not the water surface |
| "Liquid X gains heat and expands" (sealed bottle) | Accounts for the liquid only; the trapped air expands too |
| "1000 − 200 − 100 + 100" | Treats pumped-in air as adding volume; air compresses into the space available |
| "Air does not have a fixed volume" (volume of air) | A true property, but compressibility is the one doing the work |
| "The bright petals attract insects" | Stops at the first link of the chain |
| "The flower develops into a fruit" | Imprecise: the ovary develops into the fruit |
| "R has more carbon dioxide than S" | Reverses the direction; cells in the organ produce carbon dioxide continuously |
| "Gaseous exchange occurs in the small intestine" | Relocates a lung process |
| "Predators cannot spot the animal" | Absolute claim where camouflage only makes spotting harder |
| "Electricity is used up by B1 and B2" | Current-is-consumed misconception |
| "B2 takes all the current, so B3 gets none" | Parallel branches treated as competing for one supply |
| "B3 is further from the battery" | Spatial reasoning in place of circuit completeness |
| "B3 is faulty" | Ignores a condition the stem gave |
| "No bulb lights, since bulb A is wired wrongly" | Generalises a component fault to the whole circuit |
| "Both bulbs light, the circuit is closed" | Checks the loop and not the connection at each bulb |
| "The glass rod is an insulator, so no current flows" | Thinks the current passes through the core rather than the coils |
| "The glass rod becomes an electromagnet" | Current alone is enough; ignores the core material |
| "The pebble travels further because more force is used" | Answers a force question where energy conversion was asked for |
| "The pebble gains more kinetic energy" | States the destination energy without its source |
| "Plants take in the oxygen and snails take in the carbon dioxide" | Reverses the gases of photosynthesis and respiration |
| "The plants give out oxygen and the snails give out carbon dioxide" | Outcomes memorised without the two processes producing them |
| "The plants and snails exchange gases with each other" | Neither gas nor direction named |
| "More trees are cut down" | Cause restated as effect |
