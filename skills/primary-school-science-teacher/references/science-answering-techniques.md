# Science — answering open-ended questions

Everything needed to teach or mark against this file — every frame, property table, worked chain and misconception — is written out here or in the topic file this one routes to. Nothing here depends on any external document.

**This is not MOE syllabus.** It is an account of how open-ended Science answers are marked in Singapore classrooms, so it carries no authority over what a level covers, and `science.md` stays the authority on content and on every boundary. Where a frame here uses a term `science.md` records as *not required*, the boundary wins — see [Where this crosses a boundary](#where-this-crosses-a-boundary).

A child who loses marks on open-ended Science usually knows the science. What they lack is the **chain**: naming the concept that is operating, then every link from the condition given in the question to the outcome observed, in the marker's own phrasing. That is trainable, and this file and the topic files it routes to are what to train. Its topics are the upper-primary ones — heat, evaporation and condensation, matter, plant reproduction, light, digestion, circulation and respiration, food webs, adaptation, electricity, forces, energy, photosynthesis, man's impact.

Use the frames as sentence patterns, not as a word bank. The phrases must answer the question asked: a child who fits every keyword into an answer that addresses the adjacent question scores nothing.

## The shape of an answer

Five moves. They apply to every topic file, and nearly every recurring mistake is one of them missing.

**Explanation, then Result.** Name the science concept operating, then the outcome observed. Label the model answer with those two words — a child can write them as headings.

**Complete the chain.** Every link named, no leaps. *Material → heat transferred to water → water gains more heat → faster evaporation → more water lost*. *Bright petals → attract insects → pollination → fertilisation → ovary develops into fruit*. Stopping at the first link is the single most common loss of marks: "the bright petals attract insects" answers a question that was not asked.

**Compare when the question compares, and only what it asks.** Two set-ups or two materials in the stem means comparative language throughout — *a better conductor than*, *at a faster rate than*, *more carbon dioxide than*. A question asking about carbon dioxide gets carbon dioxide compared, not oxygen as well.

**Name both objects.** Heat flow needs a source and a recipient; friction needs the two surfaces in contact. "The object loses heat" and "there is frictional force" are half-answers.

**Use every condition in the stem.** "All bulbs were working properly" rules out a faulty bulb. "Placed by a window", "during the day" is what makes photosynthesis part of the answer. A number given is a number needed.

## Where this crosses a boundary

Two places where the common classroom phrasing goes beyond what `science.md` records as required. Teach the frame; drop the term.

- **Potential energy at P6.** The common classroom phrasing uses *elastic potential energy*, *gravitational potential energy* and *chemical potential energy* throughout the energy topic, and marks a child down for writing "elastic energy". The syllabus requires only **kinetic, potential, light, electrical, sound and heat** energy and records those three compound terms as not required, so *potential energy* is the term to teach and a child who writes it is not wrong. The energy frames in [`science-answering/energy-forces-and-electricity.md`](science-answering/energy-forces-and-electricity.md) are written with the boundary already applied — the usual classroom wording for the battery is *chemical potential energy* and for the stretched rubber *elastic potential energy*.
- **Stomata.** The needle-leaf frame counts "the number of stomata"; the term is not required at P5 or P6. Say *less leaf surface area, so less water vapour is lost*.

Elsewhere the same line is already policed in classroom practice, and those flags match the syllabus: *complex substances* for starch and undigested food, and for the electromagnet core, nickel and cobalt as magnetic materials and the wire being insulated — all of which sit outside PSLE. `science.md` agrees: recall of nickel and cobalt is not required at P3. Keep the flags, and keep the frames to iron and steel.

## Pitch

The frames are written for the top of the range — upper primary, Standard. Two adjustments:

- **Foundation** asks the child to *state* and *identify* where Standard asks them to *show an understanding* and *describe*. A full chain is a Standard demand; marking a Foundation script against the whole chain is marking above level. Some of this is not Foundation content at all — `science.md` has energy conversion and elastic spring force as Standard only, and the food web and the organism/population/community distinction likewise.
- These are **P4-P6** topics. The frames are a marking standard for open-ended questions, not a vocabulary a P3 child owes.
