# Paper template — practice and exam papers

The house format for any practice set or paper this skill writes, printable as it stands and markable by a parent.

It is a **formatting convention, not MOE's**: the syllabus specifies no paper structure, no section weighting and no question count for Science. The child's own school paper is the only authority on the structure in front of them — follow theirs where it differs, and keep this shape only for what it does not settle. `science.md` stays the authority on content, on every boundary, and on the command words the questions and the key are marked to.

## How to fill it

- **Header first.** Level, stream and the themes or topics covered go in the title; total marks go in the header, and they are the sum of the parts.
- **Mark every question** in square brackets at the end of the stem — `[2m]` for a part, `[5 Marks]` for a multi-part question's total.
- **Keep the sections the child sits.** Section A multiple-choice with four numbered options and bracketed answer space; Section B structured and open-ended questions. Drop a section the child's paper does not have, and rename it to match a paper that names it differently.
- **Open-ended parts get real writing space** — 4-6 blank lines, and more where the answer is a full chain.
- **Use the question's own command word** from the glossary in `science.md`, and set up the data a comparative answer needs: two set-ups, a table of readings, a labelled diagram.
- **Singapore units and contexts throughout** — °C, g, kg, mℓ, ℓ, cm, familiar plants, animals and household set-ups.
- **Answer key last**, as Part 3, always — never beside the question.

## The key is the check

Write the full chain for every open-ended question, not the final claim, in the frames the child is taught — and then read each question and each key line back against the level's boundaries. A question or a key whose own wording needs a term the syllabus records as not required is the most common failure in paper-setting.
