# English Language — Primary 1 to 6

Source: MOE *English Language Syllabus 2020 (Primary)*, Curriculum Planning and Development Division, Ministry of Education Singapore. That PDF is **not shipped with this skill** — everything needed to teach, set, mark and answer questions about primary English is in this file. The page citations (p19, p26, …) record where each claim came from for anyone auditing against the source; they are not a pointer to follow. When a user needs something this file does not carry, say it is not in the syllabus rather than filling the gap.

English is **not** a per-level topic list. It is six **Areas of Language Learning** whose skills spiral upward across three bands. Expect a question like "what does P4 English cover?" to be answered by band, not by a list of units.

**Bands** (the syllabus calls these *year levels*, p45): Lower Primary = P1-P2 · Middle Primary = P3-P4 · Upper Primary = P5-P6. There is no Standard/Foundation split.

**Areas of Language Learning (AoLL):**

| Receptive | Productive | Knowledge about language |
|---|---|---|
| Listening and Viewing · Reading and Viewing | Speaking and Representing · Writing and Representing | Grammar · Vocabulary |

Each area is organised the same way: **focus areas** → numbered **learning outcomes (LO1, LO2, …)** → sub-strands, over a content base the syllabus labels **SSAB** (skills, strategies, attitudes and behaviour) for the four skill areas and **items and structures** for Grammar and Vocabulary (p19-p30). Quote an LO by area and number — "Writing and Representing LO5" — because that is how the syllabus is indexed.

Two ideas run through the whole syllabus and change how you set and mark work:

- **Representing** is a fourth skill alongside listening, speaking, reading and writing: presenting ideas through visual, gestural, audio and spatial means, not words alone. **Viewing** is its receptive partner. So a task that asks a child to read a poster, infer from a picture, or design one, is squarely on syllabus.
- **Semiotic modes** — linguistic, visual, gestural, audio, spatial. Multimodal texts are core material, not enrichment: "texts" in this syllabus means monomodal, bimodal and multimodal texts from print, non-print and digital networked sources (p13).

Grammar and vocabulary are treated as **resources for communication**, taught both explicitly and in the context of language use — not as isolated drilling (p17). Children also learn **metalanguage** (the words for talking about language: noun, clause, tense) so they can discuss and self-correct.

## The four syllabus aims

The overarching aim is "to develop effective and affective language use in students" (p9):

1. Listen to, read and view critically and with accuracy, understanding and appreciation a wide array of literary and informational texts in standard English from print, non-print and digital networked sources.
2. Speak, write and represent in standard English that is grammatical, fluent, intelligible and appropriate for different purposes, audiences, contexts and cultures.
3. Use standard English grammar and vocabulary accurately and appropriately, and understand how speakers/writers put words together and use language to communicate meaning and achieve impact.
4. Use English with impact, effect and affect.

**Standard English** is defined in a footnote to the aims (p9): "Internationally acceptable English that is grammatical, fluent and appropriate for purpose, audience, context and culture refers to the formal register of English used in different parts of the world." That is the standard to mark against, and the reason register is a syllabus matter and not a stylistic preference.

## How the foundation is laid at lower primary

The syllabus states what the primary years front-load (p33). These are the clearest statements of sequence in the document:

- a greater focus on accuracy and fluency in oral communication (listening and speaking skills), using appropriate teaching actions for different year levels;
- enjoyment of language even as children formally learn metalanguage and grammatical items: "systematic and explicit instruction of grammar, with a focus on word, phrase and sentence level grammar before a gradual incorporation of text level grammar from upper primary to secondary levels";
- "attention to phonemic awareness, phonics and early literacy skills at the start of Primary 1 to lay the foundation for acquiring reading fluency, comprehension and viewing skills and strategies at all levels";
- attention to vocabulary development at all levels;
- an emphasis on learning-focused interaction at class and group levels;
- writing and representing skills and learner strategies for idea generation, selection, organisation, development, expression and revision, "so as to create texts with cohesion and coherence";
- greater integration between receptive and productive skills, with positive dispositions and metacognitive skills from the early years.

## How to read the band limits

The English syllabus states no "not required" prohibitions of the kind Science uses. Its limits are **level spans**: a learning outcome is printed across the year-level columns it applies to, and above-level work is work drawn from a span the child has not reached (p20-p30). Two consequences:

- The spans do not all break at band boundaries. Close reading and the Speaking knowledge base shift at **P4**, mid-band; most others shift at P3 or P5. The spans below are given in levels, so trust the levels over the band label when they disagree.
- An outcome's absence at a band is the boundary. Asking a P2 child for cohesion across paragraphs, or a P3 child to judge the credibility of a source, is asking for an outcome that starts later. Each area below closes with a **Not yet at this band** line stating that limit in the syllabus's own terms.

# Progression by band

## Listening and Viewing

*Area goal (p19):* "Develop appropriate listening and viewing attitudes and behaviour, and apply skills and strategies in a variety of contexts for effective communication and collaboration, strengthened by exposure to a wide range of listening and viewing texts."

*Focus areas:* Listening and Viewing Closely with a Positive Disposition · Listening and Viewing Critically · Listening and Viewing Extensively.

*Learning outcomes (p19):* **LO1** develop auditory discrimination skills and positive listening and viewing attitudes and behaviour · **LO2** develop listening and viewing skills for understanding by focusing on literal and inferential meaning · **LO3** develop critical listening and viewing by focusing on implied meaning, higher-order thinking and evaluation · **LO4** listen to, view and respond to a variety of texts. Sub-strands: Listening and Viewing Attitudes and Behaviour; Listening and Viewing to Recognise Sounds and Words in Context; Listening and Viewing for Understanding; Listening and Viewing for Critical Evaluation; Listening and Viewing Extensively for Understanding and Enjoyment.

Progression (p20):

- **P1-P6** — Develop a positive disposition by listening and viewing attentively for a sustained period, with empathy and respect, and by indicating response appropriately in a range of communicative contexts and interactions.
- **P1-P6** — Develop perception and recognition of sounds and words in context through phonological awareness and identification of key words, prosodic features and non-fluency features of spoken language.
- **P1-P6** — Listen and view for understanding by drawing on prior knowledge and contextual clues, including semiotic features, and applying a range of listening and viewing skills and learner strategies to interpret texts.
- **Middle Primary (P3-P4)** — Listen and view critically by making connections between parts of texts.
- **Upper Primary (P5-P6)** — Listen and view critically by analysing different semiotic features in texts, determining the credibility of sources and relevance of information, and using comprehension monitoring strategies while listening and viewing.
- **P1-P4** — Listen to, view and respond to texts for different purposes in a variety of contexts for enjoyment and understanding.
- **P5-P6** — Apply knowledge from listening to, viewing and responding to texts for different purposes (**including arguments and discussions**) in a variety of contexts for enjoyment and understanding to other areas of language learning.

**Not yet at this band** — critical listening and viewing starts at P3, and then only as *making connections between parts of texts*; analysing semiotic features, judging credibility of sources and relevance of information, and comprehension monitoring while listening are P5-P6. Arguments and discussions as listening material are named only at P5-P6.

## Reading and Viewing

*Area goal (p21):* "Develop beginning, close and critical reading and viewing skills, strategies, attitudes and behaviour to comprehend a variety of texts meaningfully, strengthened with wide and extensive reading and viewing."

*Focus areas:* Reading and Viewing Closely · Reading and Viewing Critically · Reading and Viewing Widely and Extensively for Different Purposes.

*Learning outcomes (p21):* **LO1** develop reading and viewing readiness, word recognition skills, accuracy, fluency and positive reading and viewing attitudes and behaviour · **LO2** process and comprehend age-/year level-appropriate texts by focusing on literal and inferential meaning · **LO3** analyse, evaluate and appreciate age-/year level-appropriate texts by focusing on implied meaning, higher-order thinking and judgement · **LO4** respond to a wide and extensive range of texts for enjoyment and understanding of how grammatical/lexical items and semiotic modes are used in diverse contexts. Sub-strands: Beginning Reading and Viewing; Close Reading and Viewing; Critical Reading, Viewing and Appreciation; Wide Reading and Viewing; Extensive Reading and Viewing.

Progression (p22):

- **Lower Primary (P1-P2) only** — Develop beginning reading and viewing to achieve accuracy and fluency through word recognition and acquiring a positive disposition. This is the phonics and word-recognition stage, and no beginning-reading outcome is printed beyond P2.
- **P1-P3** — Develop close reading and viewing at literal and inferential levels by using prior knowledge, contextual clues and comprehension skills and strategies.
- **P4-P6** — Strengthen close reading and viewing by using a range of comprehension, and information and media literacy skills and strategies to construct meaning from diverse and multiple texts. (The shift is at **P4**, not P5.)
- **Lower Primary (P1-P2)** — Respond to implied meaning and make personal connections with texts read and/or viewed using higher-order thinking and metacognitive strategies.
- **P3-P6** — Respond critically to and appreciate implied meaning in a variety of texts by applying analysis, judgement and metacognitive strategies, making the connections to integrate meaning in texts, and demonstrating awareness of how writers' style can influence meaning.
- **P1-P6** — Respond to a wide and extensive range of high-interest and age-appropriate texts, including selections of multimodal and hybrid texts, for different purposes — to examine the impact of different semiotic modes on text and meaning, to facilitate application of skills to other areas of language learning and subject areas, and for enjoyment.

**Setting comprehension** — the pitch of a comprehension task is set by which of those three levels of meaning it asks for. P1-P3: literal and inferential questions answerable from prior knowledge, contextual clues and the taught comprehension strategies, plus personal response to implied meaning. From P3: critical response, judgement, and integrating meaning across parts of a text. From P4: questions that require working across *diverse and multiple texts*, and information and media literacy — which source is more reliable, what the chart adds to the passage. From P5: analysis of the writer's style and its effect on meaning. Multimodal and hybrid stimulus (poster, infographic, comic, web page) is in range at every level; what changes is what you ask about it.

**Not yet at this band** — appreciation of a writer's style influencing meaning, and critical response with judgement, begin at P3; information and media literacy and multiple-text work begin at P4. The syllabus assigns nothing more complex than implied meaning and personal connection to P1-P2; *more complex and ambiguous meaning* is a secondary-level outcome and is out of range for all six primary levels.

## Speaking and Representing

*Area goal (p23):* "Develop knowledge of the features of spoken language and use speaking and representing skills, strategies, attitudes and behaviour to communicate appropriately according to purpose, audience, context and culture."

*Focus areas:* Knowledge Base for Speaking and Representing · Speaking and Representing Confidently and Effectively for a Variety of Purposes, Audiences, Contexts and Cultures, both Individually and Collaboratively.

*Learning outcomes (p23):* **LO1** develop knowledge and awareness of the features of speaking and representing · **LO2** develop accuracy and fluency in speaking and representing · **LO3** explore, generate, develop and organise ideas respectfully as individuals and collaboratively in planned and/or spontaneous situations · **LO4** speak and represent confidently, coherently and cohesively for a variety of purposes, audiences, contexts and cultures · **LO5** monitor, review, revise and refine responsibly before, during and after speaking and representing. Sub-strands: Features of Spoken Language; Accuracy and Fluency; Planning Processes (Individual, Collaborative); Speaking and Representing to Fulfil Different Purposes; Self-Monitoring.

Progression (p24):

- **P1-P3** — Develop knowledge of the basic features of spoken language and recognise that language is used to fulfil different purposes.
- **P4-P6** — Attain deeper knowledge of the features of spoken language, including variation in **register** according to situation. Recognise the conventions of speech and the roles of speakers in different situations. (The shift is at **P4**, not P5.)
- **P1-P6** — Develop fluency and accuracy in pronunciation with use of appropriate prosodic features.
- **P1-P6** — Plan and set goals.
- **P1-P3** — Explore, gather and develop ideas from a variety of texts combining linguistic and other semiotic modes.
- **P4-P6** — Gather, select and **evaluate** information. Develop and organise ideas from a variety of texts combining linguistic and other semiotic modes.
- **P1-P3** — Participate respectfully in discussion by upholding agreed-upon rules of exchange.
- **P4-P6** — Participate respectfully in discussion to develop, articulate and represent ideas in real time in response to the listener(s).
- **P1-P6** — Speak and represent with confidence, coherence and cohesion using different semiotic modes appropriately to fulfil different purposes.
- **Text purposes for speaking** — P1-P4: "Pay increasing attention to producing texts that **recount, entertain, instruct and respond**." P5-P6: "Pay increasing attention to producing texts that **recount, entertain, instruct, describe, inform, respond and evaluate**."
- **Self-monitoring** — P1-P4: Monitor self and others in order to correct mispronounced words and adjust inappropriate use of prosodic features. P5-P6: Self-adjust planned speech, monitor and revise speech to adjust and improve communication based on intended purposes and response of the listener, immediately after and upon reflection.

**Not yet at this band** — register variation, the conventions of speech and the roles of speakers, and evaluating gathered information all begin at P4. Describing, informing and evaluating as spoken text purposes begin at P5; arguing and persuading as spoken purposes are secondary outcomes and are out of range across primary. Before P4, discussion is assessed on upholding the agreed rules of exchange, not on responding to a listener in real time.

## Writing and Representing

*Area goal (p25):* "Develop a positive disposition towards writing and representing, writing readiness and handwriting, spelling accuracy, and apply skills and strategies for idea generation, selection, organisation, development, expression and revision in creating a variety of texts, to address different purposes, audiences, contexts and cultures."

*Focus areas:* Acquiring the Mechanics of Writing · Writing and Representing Creatively and Critically for a Variety of Purposes, Audiences, Contexts and Cultures, both Individually and Collaboratively.

*Learning outcomes (p25):* **LO1** develop writing readiness and handwriting to write accurately and fluently · **LO2** use accurate and consistent spelling · **LO3** generate, select and organise ideas for writing and representing creatively and critically · **LO4** develop, organise and express ideas coherently, cohesively, creatively and critically to produce texts for a variety of purposes, audiences, contexts and cultures · **LO5** review, revise, edit and proofread to improve writing and representing. Sub-strands: Handwriting; Spelling; Generation, Selection and Organisation of Ideas; Development, Organisation and Expression of Ideas; Review, Revision and Editing.

Progression (p26):

- **P1-P6** — Develop a positive disposition towards writing and representing.
- **Lower Primary (P1-P2)** — Develop writing readiness and write in print script. Apply spelling skills and strategies accurately for writing age-/year level-appropriate words.
- **P3-P6** — Write neatly, legibly and fluently. Apply spelling skills and learner strategies accurately for writing **frequently misspelled and multisyllabic words**.
- **Lower Primary (P1-P2)** — Learn to write and represent by generating, selecting, organising, developing, expressing and revising ideas.
- **P3-P6** — Apply skills for idea generation, selection, organisation, development, expression and revision so as to address the writer's purpose, needs of the audience, context and culture in writing and representing. Pay increasing attention to the use of semiotic modes in text creation.
- **P1-P6** — Create a variety of texts for different purposes, using an appropriate tone and register. Understand how the skills and knowledge of writing and representing specific types of texts can inform and be applied to the creation of other increasingly sophisticated texts of different types and/or forms.
- **Lower Primary (P1-P2)** — Learn to write and represent with **some attention** to the appropriate organisational structures and language features of texts for different purposes.
- **Middle Primary (P3-P4)** — Write and represent with an **explicit awareness** of the appropriate organisational structures and language features of texts for different purposes.
- **Upper Primary (P5-P6)** — **Apply knowledge** of appropriate organisational structures and language features to create a variety of increasingly sophisticated texts for different purposes.
- **Upper Primary (P5-P6)** — Pay increasing attention to writing and representing texts that **respond, argue, evaluate and/or persuade**.
- Keyboarding and word processing skills are supported alongside handwriting (p26, footnote: "Support also the use of keyboarding and word processing skills to create texts").

**Setting composition** — the three-step ladder *some attention → explicit awareness → apply knowledge* of organisational structure and language features is the spine of composition pitch. P1-P2: write to a purpose with the structure modelled for them. P3-P4: the child should be able to name and use the structure of the text type being written, and address purpose, audience, context and culture. P5-P6: structure and language features applied independently across a variety of text types, including texts that respond, argue, evaluate or persuade. Revision is part of the outcome at every level, so build planning and redrafting into the task rather than treating the first draft as the product.

**Not yet at this band** — print script and age-appropriate spelling are the P1-P2 mechanics; frequently misspelled and multisyllabic words begin at P3. Argument, evaluation and persuasion as *written* purposes are Upper Primary; asking a P3 child for a persuasive essay is above-level, though responding to a text is in range from P1. Creating hybrid texts deliberately is a secondary outcome.

## Grammar

*Area goal (p27):* "Develop knowledge of grammar for the purposeful use of language at the word, phrase, sentence and text levels."

*Focus areas:* Use of Terms · Grammar at Word, Phrase and Sentence Levels · Grammar at Text Level.

*Learning outcomes (p27):* **LO1** use metalanguage for learning and talking about language structures and language in use · **LO2** apply knowledge of grammatical rules at word and phrase levels · **LO3** apply knowledge of grammatical rules at sentence level · **LO4** show understanding of how the purposeful use of language shapes meaning in texts. Sub-strands: Metalanguage; At Word and Phrase Levels; At Sentence Level; Cohesion in Texts; Language Features of Types of Texts.

Progression (p28):

- **Lower Primary (P1-P2)** — Learn metalanguage explicitly to talk about how language works at word and phrase levels and for identification of simple sentence functions in meaningful contexts.
- **P3-P6** — Use metalanguage to talk about how language works at the **text level** and during editing and self-correction.
- **P1-P6** — Apply grammatical knowledge at the word, phrase and sentence levels to convey ideas accurately in different social contexts. Understand that by varying the forms of construction, different meanings are conveyed in different contexts.
- **P3-P6 only** — Use **cohesive devices** and grammatical structures to create links across different clauses, sentences and paragraphs. Identify and apply knowledge of language features in different types of texts during speaking, writing and representing. Recognise how the purposeful use of language shapes meaning in texts.

**Not yet at this band** — Grammar at Text Level is printed from P3 onwards and nothing in that focus area is assigned to P1-P2, so asking a P1 or P2 child about cohesion across paragraphs, or about language features of a text type, is above-level. Their grammar work is word and phrase level plus simple sentence functions. The syllabus also sets the order of attack directly: word, phrase and sentence level grammar first, "before a gradual incorporation of text level grammar from upper primary to secondary levels" (p33) — so treat text-level grammar as emerging at P3-P4 and consolidating at P5-P6, not as a P3 mastery target.

The syllabus does **not** list the grammatical items and structures for each level; it names "items and structures" as the content base (p27) without enumerating them. If a user asks which tenses or structures are prescribed at a level, say the syllabus does not specify them and work from their school's scheme of work or textbook.

## Vocabulary

*Area goal (p29):* "Develop knowledge of vocabulary for the purposeful use of rich language."

*Focus areas:* Developing Rich Vocabulary Knowledge · Using Vocabulary.

*Learning outcomes (p29):* **LO1** develop word consciousness and use metalanguage in building vocabulary knowledge · **LO2** build rich vocabulary knowledge that supports the development of listening, reading, viewing, speaking, writing and representing skills · **LO3** use words appropriate for purpose, audience, context and culture. Sub-strands: Use of Metalanguage; Development of Rich Vocabulary; Use of Appropriate Vocabulary. The content base is items, structures **and learner strategies**.

Progression (p30):

- **Lower Primary (P1-P2)** — Focus on learning words through experience and enjoyment. Develop vocabulary knowledge and take an active role in learning new vocabulary items.
- **P3-P6** — Develop and strengthen vocabulary knowledge, and take an active role in learning new vocabulary items.
- **P1-P6** — Develop rich vocabulary knowledge by examining how words are formed, how words relate to one another and how words are used in context. Use various learner strategies to develop rich vocabulary.
- **P1-P6** — Use words suitable for purpose, audience, context and culture. Use fixed expressions accurately and appropriately. Recognise, appreciate and use words for literary effect meaningfully. Use words meaningfully in conjunction with semiotic modes.

**Not yet at this band** — Vocabulary is the one area with almost no level gating: every *Using Vocabulary* outcome is printed across all six primary levels, so word choice for purpose and audience, fixed expressions, and words used for literary effect are all fair ground from P1. What changes with band is the sophistication of the words themselves ("age-/year level-appropriate") and, at P1-P2, the framing — words are learnt through experience and enjoyment. The syllabus prescribes no word lists or word counts at any level.

# Types of texts

"The purpose and context of a text determine its type" (p45). The suggested range of texts to be used **across all areas of language learning and year levels** (p15-p16):

- texts that **entertain**
- texts that **recount** what happened
- texts that **instruct**
- texts that **describe and inform**
- texts that **explain**
- texts that **respond, argue, evaluate and/or persuade**
- texts that contain more than one type and form of texts (**hybrid texts**)

The glossary's examples of types of texts are "personal recounts, factual recounts, narratives, descriptive reports and arguments" (p45). The familiar classroom forms for each purpose — narrative, poem and playscript to entertain; procedure and set of directions to instruct; information report to inform; book or film review to respond — are classroom practice rather than syllabus wording, so use them as forms a child will recognise, not as a list the syllabus prescribes.

The list is a range for all levels, but the bands weight it: *recount, entertain, instruct, respond* are named for speaking at P1-P4, with *describe, inform, evaluate* added at P5-P6 (p24), and writing texts that *respond, argue, evaluate and/or persuade* is an Upper Primary outcome (p26).

Teachers make "judicious selection of texts with a variety of themes on Singaporean, Asian, contemporary and international topics and perspectives", so children can broaden their worldviews and connect to real-world issues (p15).

# Pedagogy

The syllabus names two frameworks, both drawn from the Singapore Teaching Practice:

- **CLLIPS** — the six principles of EL teaching and learning (p35): **C**ontextualisation (learning tasks set in authentic, meaningful contexts; lessons planned around learning outcomes, a theme or a type of text), **L**earner-centredness (differentiating by readiness, interests and learning profiles), **L**earning-focused Interaction (a rich, responsive environment that explicitly fosters oral communication), **I**ntegration (receptive skills, productive skills, grammar and vocabulary taught in an integrated way, one set of skills building on another), **P**rocess Orientation (the teacher modelling, scaffolding and differentiating the process, not just collecting the product), **S**piral Progression (instruct, revise and revisit skills, items, structures and text types at increasing levels of difficulty).
- **ACoLADE** — the EL teaching processes across a lesson (p36): raising **A**wareness (activate prior knowledge, make connections), structuring **Co**nsolidation (revisit and reinforce), facilitating Assessment for **L**earning (identify readiness, monitor, give timely and useful feedback), enabling **A**pplication (teach language in authentic contexts of use, model it, let children work collaboratively), guiding **D**iscovery (prompt and question so the child reaches the rule without being told), instructing **E**xplicitly (explain and clarify a skill, strategy or process directly and systematically).

Three practical consequences:

- **Scaffolding** is the core move: the child works on a collaborative task with an experienced other, who demonstrates, supports and guides, then withdraws that support gradually as the child becomes independent and able to work without help (p44). This maps directly onto how to tutor — model, then joint construction, then independent writing.
- **Exploratory talk** and collaborative learning in pairs or groups, driven by questioning techniques, thinking routines, and inquiry through dialogue (p15). So prefer asking the child a question over telling them the answer, and treat *Guiding Discovery* as the default before *Instructing Explicitly*.
- **Integration** means a task that spans areas is more on-syllabus than one that drills a single area: read or view a text, discuss it, then write in response.

# Assessing and marking

From the syllabus (p39-p41):

- Align assessment with the requirements and **learning outcomes** of the syllabus: decide the learning outcomes to be attained for each year level and area of language learning, then assess those.
- Assess both **formally and informally**, at a frequency decided by the school, using different modes, so that a wide range of skills, strategies, attitudes and behaviour is developed and items and structures are learnt.
- "While assessment at upper primary may converge on a narrower range of specific learning outcomes, assessment at lower and mid-primary, which can be formal as well as informal, could cover a more varied range of learning outcomes" (p40). So a P1-P4 task may legitimately range across areas; a P5-P6 task is expected to target fewer outcomes more precisely.
- A balanced plan assesses language skills and knowledge "in a valid, reliable and holistic manner", and considers the purpose of the assessment, a wide range of modes and tasks matched to learners, the time needed to acquire and practise a skill before it is assessed, the quality and use of feedback, specifications that align assessment with teaching, and "the use of holistic or analytic scoring where most appropriate".
- Formative and summative assessment "are not separate constructs" — use both to inform teaching.
- Teach the child to **review, revise, edit and proofread** their own work: it is Writing and Representing LO5, so build self-editing into feedback rather than correcting everything for them. Grammar metalanguage is explicitly for use "during editing and self-correction" from P3 (p28), which makes naming the rule part of the mark, not an aside.
- Mark against **standard English** as the syllabus defines it (p9): grammatical, fluent, intelligible, and appropriate for purpose, audience, context and culture. Aim 2 makes appropriateness to purpose and audience part of accuracy, not a separate nicety.
- For reading aloud, the syllabus's definition of **fluency** is the criterion (p43): "reading at a level of accuracy and rate where decoding is relatively effortless and oral reading is smooth, with correct stress and intonation, and where attention can be allocated to comprehension."
- For spoken work, the assessable features named across Speaking and Representing are pronunciation and prosodic features (stress, intonation, volume, pace), fluency and accuracy, coherence and cohesion, appropriate use of semiotic modes, and self-monitoring (p23-p24).

**The syllabus prescribes no paper format, duration, mark weighting, word count or marking rubric at any level** — it sets learning outcomes and leaves assessment design to schools. If a user asks for PSLE or school-paper specifications, say they are not in the syllabus, then work from the paper, rubric or past script the user has in front of them.

The following is **classroom practice**, not syllabus wording — it is the usual Singapore primary marking frame, and is sound because each criterion maps onto a learning outcome, but do not quote it as MOE:

- For a composition, comment on content, language and organisation: whether the purpose and audience were addressed (Writing LO3-LO4); organisational structure appropriate to the text type (the *some attention / explicit awareness / apply knowledge* ladder); idea development; vocabulary choice (Vocabulary LO3); grammatical accuracy (Grammar LO2-LO3); and mechanics — spelling, punctuation, handwriting (Writing LO1-LO2).
- Singlish features (*lah*, *got*, "can or not", missing articles and plural markers, tense inconsistency) are best flagged as **register** issues, naming the standard form rather than the error alone. The syllabus itself makes no mention of Singlish; what it gives you is the standard-English and register definitions above, which is the honest ground to stand on with a parent.

# Glossary

Definitions are the syllabus's own (p43-p45), condensed.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Assessment for Learning | assessment with a formative purpose — used to give useful feedback to teachers and students that can improve both teaching and learning; contrasted with assessment *of* learning, which is summative and certifies learning for reporting to stakeholders |
| assessment task | an activity set to collect learning achievement data — for communicating findings, planning further tasks, and improving teaching and learning |
| digital networked sources | a communication system linking multiple users through a digital network, e.g. the Internet, learning management systems |
| explicit instruction | the teacher models and explains the specific strategies being learnt, gives guided practice and feedback on their use, and promotes independent practice in applying them |
| fluency | reading at a level of accuracy and rate where decoding is relatively effortless and oral reading is smooth, with correct stress and intonation, and attention can be allocated to comprehension |
| functions of language | language's major functions — descriptive, social, expressive, and textual (for creating written and spoken texts) |
| hybrid texts | texts that combine and contain more than one type of text and form, typically mixing elements from different sources or genres; infotainment is an example, aiming both to inform and to entertain |
| information literacy | the ability to access and evaluate information from different sources, and to use it meaningfully and effectively |
| informational texts | texts about real people, places and events, largely giving factual information to readers, e.g. documentaries |
| learning outcome | an expected attainment target achieved as a result of teacher instruction; it specifies the desired result, not the input. To show an outcome is attained, the child demonstrates mastery of basic language skills, learner strategies, attitudes and behaviour, and items and structures, measurable through informal or formal assessment tasks |
| literary language | deliberate constructions and choices of language a writer uses to convey, reinforce and enhance meaning in writing of a literary nature, e.g. similes, metaphors |
| literary texts | texts that relate an event, a series of events or a story; a literary text can be imaginary, as in a short story |
| media literacy | the ability to access, analyse, evaluate and create information in a variety of forms and media |
| metacognition | knowledge of the cognitive processes used in learning — planning, monitoring and evaluating learning before, during and after it — so as to decide what to focus on, refine or repair |
| multimodal | use of more than one mode of communication — linguistic, visual, gestural, audio, spatial — in a single text to convey meaning, with connections made between the modes |
| organisational structure | how information is sequenced and organised in a text type; this structure creates the coherence in a text |
| prosodic features | speech features extending across more than one speech sound — stress, intonation, volume, pace |
| register | the variety of language used in specific social situations, chosen on the formality or informality of the context and varying with the type of situation, setting, participants and topic |
| representing | the active process of applying skills and strategies to present facts, ideas and points of view through texts with linguistic, visual, gestural, audio and spatial semiotic features |
| scaffolding | a collaborative task or interaction with an experienced other, during which demonstrations, support, guidance and input are provided and then gradually withdrawn as the student becomes independent and able to work without help |
| semiotic modes | the modes of communication — linguistic, visual, gestural, audio, spatial — used to understand, interpret, analyse and construct meaning in a text |
| task | an activity designed to help students acquire or develop a specific skill, learner strategy, attitude, behaviour, item and/or structure, or a learning outcome |
| text | literary and informational texts that are well written and engaging, rich in content, concerned with a variety of themes, topics, ideas and issues; monomodal, bimodal or multimodal, from diverse sources |
| theme | in non-fiction, the main idea of the piece; in literature, the dominating idea or implicit message — seldom stated directly, and inferred by the reader or viewer |
| tone | the mood or atmosphere of a work; also the writer's attitude to the reader (formal, informal) or treatment of the subject matter (light, serious) |
| topic | what a text is about — its subject or focus |
| type of text | determined by purpose and context, e.g. personal recounts, factual recounts, narratives, descriptive reports, arguments |
| viewing | the active process of applying skills and learner strategies to interpret and understand texts that use linguistic, visual, gestural and spatial semiotic features |
| visual literacy | the ability to construct meaning from symbols and images, and to communicate through visual means |
| year levels | Lower Primary = P1-P2 · Middle Primary = P3-P4 · Upper Primary = P5-P6 (Lower Secondary = S1-S2, Upper Secondary = S3-S5) |
